notes: |
  THE SHOWCASE — 4 min. `reveal: replace`, so each step swaps the panel
  rather than stacking it; arrow DOWN between them. One sentence per step,
  then let them look.

  No panel names a height: every one of them grows into whatever the slide
  has left, so this reads the same fullscreen, in a window the size the
  window manager felt like, and at any zoom.

  1 — core. Everything here is `react-x11` itself: no components package.
      Drag the weight slider and say "those are the font file's own axes,
      read off the machine this is running on" — then type in the field and
      let the specimen and the vector move together.
      CAREFUL: clicking the field gives it the keyboard, so `space` types a
      space. `Esc`, or click the background, hands it back.
  2 — three documents behind one strip of tabs. Land the HTML tab: that
      `<select>` is not a picture of a menu, it is the same menu. Switch
      tabs and back to show the panels stayed alive.
  3 — the chart. The commit-history numbers drawn as an area instead of bars, so
      nothing new is being claimed here; the component is the point.
  4 — the timeline. The one thing on this slide that registers no element:
      box, text, and one absolutely-positioned pixel down the gutter.
  5 — the architecture, and the slide's best three minutes if you have
      them. Say what the graph says — five stages from setState to the
      wire, the backend swapped in one place — then OPERATE it: flip the
      backend radio and watch a branch go, turn the paint cache off and
      watch the edge start marching, tick a desktop hook and the D-Bus
      wing appears. Those are real widgets inside a drawn graph, which is
      the seam worth naming. The pane's own controls are bottom-left and
      the minimap bottom-right: the third control button re-frames the
      graph after you have dragged something into the weeds. The wheel
      zooms, and under 0.6 the form nodes stop mounting, which is the
      budget that makes drawing the default.
      The bottom wing is this deck: `useBadge` reaches the Dock without
      touching yoga, paint or the X connection.
  6 — the map is LIVE over the network. If the room's wifi is out it says
      so and draws the style's background — that is fine, say it out loud
      and move on.
  7 — GL through `<glarea>`, and the best closing beat on the slide. Drive
      the scene from the bar: spin to 0 and back, wireframe on, swap the
      shape, open the colour field. Then hit the SOURCE tab — that is the
      file this demo is running from, read off disk, not a snippet pasted
      into a slide. Point at the props in it and at the widgets that were
      moving them. If somebody asks: yes, editing the file and pressing
      `r` updates the listing.

  If you are behind schedule, cut to steps 1, 5 and 6: core, the
  architecture and the two hard ones carry the claim, and 2, 3 and 4 are
  the ones people ask about after.
